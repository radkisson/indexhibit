Boardmusic — What the Water Taught Me

2026-07-01 · EXHIBIT

About

A musical performance conducted while floating on a paddleboard on open water, Canada Day 2026, at a public beach. The performer ran three interfaces at once: vocal improvisation, a hydraulophone (continuous, fluid melody), and a mobile touchscreen DAW (discrete rhythm). The performance was itself the experiment — a stress test of what environmental instability does to fundamentally different interface paradigms.

What the water taught

Waves shift the performer's center of gravity continuously. The touchscreen, encapsulated to survive water, loses its tactile link exactly when visual targeting is hardest — protection makes it fragile. The hydraulophone is of the water: it needs water to function, so the environment and the instrument are one material. The paddleboard and lake fold into the performer's body schema (Merleau-Ponty via Mann's Mersivity: reciprocal immersibility).
