---
title: Spatial Audio
date: 2026-08-18
---

A room has two acoustic regimes separated by the Schroeder frequency $f_s$. Below $f_s$, standing-wave modes dominate: pressure varies point to point, and "fixing" the room means moving modes (bass traps, resonators, placement). Above $f_s$, geometric acoustics dominates: reverb, reflections, diffusion. Confusing the regimes is how people buy foam that does nothing.

Worked case (5 × 4 × 3 m, $V = 60\,\text{m}^3$): fundamental mode 34.3 Hz on the long axis ($\lambda = 10$ m), $f_s \approx 140$–$200$ Hz depending on treatment. Treatment: corner bass traps (80–300 Hz), Helmholtz panels (68 Hz cluster), EQ notch (34–43 Hz). Verified with a WolframScript toolkit.

The regime split is also a listening lesson for [[listening-as-method]]: what you hear in a room depends on which regime you are standing in. It underlies the environment-reading in [[boardmusic]].
