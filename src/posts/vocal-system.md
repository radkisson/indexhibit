## About

I keep a 46-song reference document that maps every piece in my working repertoire against my vocal profile. It is not a list of songs I like; it is an instrument manual.

My voice sits at the high baritone / rock tenor boundary with a countertenor extension. Physiological range: G2 to C6, including falsetto. Rock mix and scream can reach G5 or Ab5, but those are rare-use events with a 24 to 48 hour recovery window attached. The tessitura for jazz and crooner work sits comfortably between G2 and D4, with warm chest resonance and a close-mic distance of two to four inches.

The system is organized into three modes that do not blend freely. Jazz and crooner mode means staying below the passaggio (Eb4 to F4), keeping the mic close, and never sustaining notes in the break zone. Rock mode removes those safety limits: belt through the passaggio, push the distance to six to twelve inches, and accept the recovery cost. Countertenor mode is a separate instrument entirely, requiring straight tone, precise onset, and focused head voice. Switching between modes is a deliberate act, not a slide.

## The 46-song reference

The repertoire is divided into six sections: swing and up-tempo (songs like "All of Me" in Bb, ranging from Bb2 to D4), ballads and dark jazz ("Misty" in D, A2 to D4), bossa nova and Latin ("Girl from Ipanema" in Db, Ab2 to Eb4), rock and belt encore ("Gethsemane" from Jesus Christ Superstar in Bb minor, pushing from Bb2 up to G5), falsetto features ("Nature Boy" in E minor), and Baroque and classical countertenor material ("The Cold Song" in C minor, F3 to F5).

Each entry records key, exact range, and which mode it requires. A 60-minute jazz set draws from sections one through three, keeping strict G2 to D4 behavior. Rock material (section four) only goes into a set when the voice is fresh and the gig calls for it. Classical countertenor pieces are special features, not filler.

## Recovery and logistics

Fifteen-plus minutes of warm-up is non-negotiable. After any rock scream usage, the clock starts: 24 to 48 hours before the upper range is reliable again. Morning voice actually enhances the bottom end (G2 to A2), which is useful for sound checks but irrelevant for performance.

The document lives as a quick-reference export from a larger spreadsheet system. The spreadsheet tracks practice status, risk level per song, and recovery scheduling. The 46-song reference is what gets printed and handed to a sound engineer or setlist collaborator: key, range, mode, done.
