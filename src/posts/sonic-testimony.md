## About

Sonic Testimony is an original theory of art developed for Humber PHIL 2015 (Theories of Beauty). The core claim: art is vibration that enters the body before meaning arrives, and what it creates is not an audience but witnesses.

## The Theory

The theory argues that art's primary channel is not meaning but vibration. It enters the body before cognition can process it. What it produces is not an audience (passive receivers of meaning) but witnesses: bodies that have been entered by the vibration.

This is grounded in four thinkers:

- **Tolstoy**: the infectious transmission of feeling through voice.
- **Korsmeyer**: the sensory hierarchy that privileges sight over sound and touch.
- **Sontag**: anti-interpretation; the demand to let the work act on the senses before the intellect classifies it.
- **Ashcroft**: material resonance; sound as physical event, not symbol.

## From a Singer's Body

The script grounded the theory in personal experience as a singer. The voice is the instrument where body rotation becomes sound in real time. Performance analysis led to a key realization: "I'm not expressing an idea I had in advance. I'm finding out what my body already knows."

This distinction is the hinge. The body knows before the mind names. Sonic testimony names that gap.

## For Something vs. As Content

The theory contrasts two modes:

- Music **for** something: instrumental, message-driven, the sound serves an external meaning.
- Sound **as** content: the vibration itself is the work. No translation layer.

Sonic testimony belongs to the second category. The witness does not decode a message. The witness has been physically altered by the encounter.

## Materials

The PHIL 2015 submission included an audio recording, a full written script, and a written reflection. The theory was developed through the practice of performing and then analyzing what happened during performance.
