## About

In spring 2026 I produced a nine-note research podcast for Humber's MUS 1001 (History of Music II) under Prof. Brad Klump. The working title was *Archiving the Margins: Cueca Chora in Chile's National Memory*, and the central thesis landed as follows:

> Memoria Chilena domesticates cueca chora by branding marginality as heritage.

The project did not use Memoria Chilena (Chile's state-funded digital heritage archive) as a neutral source. Instead, I treated the archive's own entries as primary texts: the language it uses to describe cueca chora ("urban invention," "nocturnal space," "marginality," "bajos fondos," "hybrid musical creation") is itself the object of study.

## The Argument

The archive describes cueca chora through a vocabulary of danger and colour, then slots that description into a national-memory framework. The result: class conflict, urban violence, political repression, and the embodied roughness of the music get recast as aesthetic flavour. Poverty becomes a genre marker. The *bajos fondos* become consumable heritage.

The podcast traces this through a few key concepts. **Curated marginality**: the practice of selecting, framing, and displaying marginal culture from inside an institution. **Aestheticized poverty**: the transformation of real exclusion into mood, texture, or brand. **Domestication**: the process by which a disruptive, dangerous practice becomes safe cultural property.

## Why It Matters

The tension is real. Recognition preserves music that could otherwise be erased; but it also freezes, classifies, and sanitizes. Cueca chora was always mediated by class, industry, family networks, performance circuits, and institutions. What the official archive does is collapse that contradictory history into a single word: "marginal." That word then becomes the genre's essence, and the genre becomes a heritage brand.

The podcast argues that this is not a conspiracy. It is what state archives do. But naming the mechanism matters, because it clarifies what is lost when a living, class-marked, politically charged practice becomes an object in a digital display case.
