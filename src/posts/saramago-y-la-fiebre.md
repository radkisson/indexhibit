## About

Saramago y La Fiebre is a Toronto-based ensemble that works inside bolero noir, jazz guachaca, and manouche rock de puerto. The instrumentation is voice (libre), guitar, piano or Rhodes, contrabajo or bajo eléctrico, batería or percussion, and a sixth voice that rotates: violin, accordion, clarinet. Tagline: "Canciones elegantes portándose mal."

The project is not a band built around a frontperson. Saramago is the container; the ensemble fills it. The precedent is the Angel Parra Trio, where the singer held the space and the band carried the rhythmic motor without competing for the lyrical spotlight.

## Twelve Conversations

**1. Saramago as container.** The name does not belong to the singer. It names the room the music happens in. The ensemble exists inside it.

**2. Voice as free agent.** The vocalist is not the bandleader. The band carries the motor ritmico; the voice enters and leaves at will, like a horn.

**3. Sextet for palette.** Six instruments exist to access five genres: boleros, manouche, rock de puerto, cueca, salsa oscura. Each song picks its subset.

**4. Bolero as tension.** Bolero is not about abandonment. It is about the space between desire and dignity. The arrangement holds that space open.

**5. Latin Noir as lighting.** Not a genre. A light condition. Intimate, nocturnal, suspicious, dry, sexual through proximity rather than declaration.

**6. Two guitars, two logics.** Carlos Santana plays heat and sustain. Gabor Szabo plays smoke and silence. Both are present in the ensemble, never simultaneously.

**7. Los Tres as elasticity.** A band that could switch genres mid-set without switching identity. Saramago borrows this: the song changes, the sound remains recognizable.

**8. Tarkany Muevek as density.** Instruments enter and exit by phrase. There is no tutti default. The arrangement breathes by subtraction.

**9. Not world music.** Everything passes through Chilean performance practice first. The bolero, the manouche, the jazz, all filtered through how Chilean musicians play.

**10. Song is center.** Arrangement exists to serve the wound in the song, not to display compositional technique.

**11. Desire versus dignity.** The lyric stance: "I know you see me. I know you could. You have chosen worse." Elegance is the weapon.

**12. Chronology as architecture.** If the band launches live before recording, Toronto defines what Saramago sounds like. The order of events shapes the sound.

## Portarse Mal

The creative principle is not genre fusion. It is songs that behave elegantly, then break the rule they just established. A bolero that ends in a manouche turnaround. A cueca rhythm under a jazz harmony. The misbehavior is the point. The elegance makes it land.
